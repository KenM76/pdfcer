**Write every user-facing reply as a TL;DR — "Coles Notes".** Not just when
asked, and not just for summaries. This is the default register for all
conversation with Ken.

**Why:** Ken, 2026-08-20, after a long detailed session report followed by a
short one: *"omg that is so much better. how do I get you to tldr coles notes
all the time."*

★ **AND IT IS NOW A GLOBAL RULE, NOT A pdfce ONE.** When it was first recorded
here and in pdfce's session log only, he came back with *"put it in the global
rules please."* It is the **first section** of `C:/Users/Ken/.claude/CLAUDE.md`,
above every other directive, and applies to **every project on the machine** —
SolidWorks, ScripTree, combridge, tax, all of them.

**This file is the pdfce-local copy of a global instruction. If the two ever
disagree, the global file wins.**

**The shape that worked**, reproduced so it can be copied:

- **A bolded claim per item**, written as an outcome in his terms — *"Fixed a
  bug that was silently wrecking your CAD files"* — not as a component name.
- **One or two plain sentences** under it. What it means for him, not how it
  works.
- **Six items maximum.** Group everything else under one "housekeeping" line.
- **End with what's next**, in a few words.
- No tables unless the data genuinely is tabular. No section headers for a
  reply under a page. No commit hashes, Pass IDs, file paths or clause numbers
  unless he asked for one.

**How to apply:**

- The detail still gets WRITTEN — into commit messages, `ROADMAP.md`,
  `SESSION_LOG.md`, doc comments and the FeatureRequests replies. Those are
  where thoroughness belongs, and the documentation-first rule is untouched.
  **The chat reply is not the archive.** That distinction is the whole trick:
  brevity to him, completeness to the record.
- Length correlates with how much I did, and it should not. A 22-commit day and
  a one-fix day get the same reply length.
- When something genuinely needs depth — a decision he must make, a risk, a
  number he asked for — give it, but say it in the fewest words that carry it,
  and keep the surrounding items short.
- If he asks a direct question, answer it first, in one line, before anything
  else.
